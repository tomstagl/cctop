The strongest rework/outcome signals found in the ecosystem are deterministic and cheap: interruption rate ("[Request interrupted by user" — sniffly, session-meta), categorised tool errors (sniffly's 15 regex classes; Claude Code's 6 substring classes), rephrase storms and retry loops via string similarity (session-signals), consecutive-failure cascades, permission denials, long stalls, and edit accept/reject rate (OTel `code_edit_tool.decision`, Cursor/Copilot/Claude org analytics "acceptance rate"). The biggest token-saving gaps versus the field are cache-cold countdown and re-cache cost, cache-invalidation event detection (model/effort/fast-mode switch, non-deferred MCP connect, bare deny rule, image-batch drop), per-turn usage attribution from the transcript's `attribution*` keys, a daily/all-sessions $ total, 5-hour-block projection against a personal historical max or P90, human-vs-model wall-clock split, and hard budget/kill-switch semantics (burnstop, ccboard). cctop already covers, better than anyone else, live per-tool context cost, compaction forecasting, subagent/MCP process state and rate-limit exhaustion projection; the gaps are almost all one new data source (statusline `prompt_cache`, session-meta/facets, hook decisions) plus a handful of string-match rules.
